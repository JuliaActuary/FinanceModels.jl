FinanceModels.Fit.Bootstrap — Type
Bootstrap()

A singleton type passed to fit to bootstrap a spline curve one quote at a time. Each step solves for the zero rate at the next quote maturity to match its price.

Supports Spline.Linear() (equivalently Spline.PolynomialSpline(1) or Spline.BSpline(1)). Bootstrapping requires that adding a knot on the right leaves every earlier curve segment unchanged, so earlier quotes stay exactly priced. Linear interpolation of zero rates has this property; quadratic, cubic, higher-order B-spline, PCHIP, and Akima interpolation do not, because a later knot changes the shape of earlier segments. Fit those strategies across the complete quote set with Fit.Loss(x -> x^2), and fit a monotone convex curve with fit(Spline.MonotoneConvex(), quotes).

After solving, every quote is repriced on the returned curve; a residual beyond root-finder precision throws an ArgumentError naming the quote.

A subtype of FitMethod.

Examples

quotes = ZCBPrice([0.99, 0.97, 0.94])
curve = fit(Spline.Linear(), quotes, Fit.Bootstrap())
discount(curve, 2) ≈ 0.97 # true

FinanceModels.Fit.LeastSquares — Type
Fit.LeastSquares(; weights = nothing, maxiters = 200)

Fit a model by weighted least squares: the parameters that minimize

Σᵢ wᵢ (present_value(model, qᵢ.instrument) - qᵢ.price)²

over the quotes qᵢ, with wᵢ = 1 when weights is nothing. It applies to splines, knot curves and every model with variables, and to the contracts whose prices under the model ForwardDiff can differentiate twice:

fit(model, quotes, Fit.LeastSquares())                     # the model's `variables`
fit(Spline.Cubic(), quotes, Fit.LeastSquares())            # one knot per quote maturity
fit(ZeroRateCurve(rates, knots, spline), quotes, Fit.LeastSquares())   # the curve's knots

The last form fits a few knots to many quotes, whose maturities may repeat: ten knots to the thousand bonds of an index, for example. Like every knot-curve fit it starts from FinanceModels' standard initial rates, not the supplied curve's.

weights holds one finite, non-negative number per quote. A weight of zero leaves its quote out: it is not priced, and in a spline fit it gets no knot. With D a bond's modified duration, weights of 1 / (price * D)^2 make each weighted residual approximately a yield error, so that long bonds do not dominate the fit.

The solve has two phases, neither specific to a model or a contract:

  1. Levenberg–Marquardt on the Jacobian of the pricing residuals (ForwardDiff, first derivatives only) takes the parameters to where a Gauss–Newton step is at rounding, or no longer shrinks. maxiters limits its iterations.
  2. Newton's method with the exact Hessian of the sum of squares, which includes the residuals' curvature, goes on from there to a point it can certify as a local minimum, and that point is the fit. Its Hessian is positive definite and well conditioned, and its Newton correction is tested one parameter and one quote at a time. The estimated price move must be at most 1e-10 times the larger of the absolute quoted and current model prices, subject to the FX outright allowance below. A quote priced at zero also includes its absolute starting value in that scale. Across the full correction, the change in each quote's pricing gradient, multiplied by the square root of its weight, must have norm at most 1e-8 in the inverse Hessian metric of half the weighted sum of squares. The solver takes further Newton steps when the gradients change more than this. These local tests estimate the accuracy of the returned parameters.

A fit that reprices its quotes is the zero-residual case of the same solve. A fit that cannot be certified throws a FitConvergenceError:

  • The quotes must determine every parameter. A parameter on which neither the prices nor their curvature depend throws an ArgumentError naming it. At an exact fit the pricing Jacobian must have full column rank. With nonzero residuals their curvature can also identify parameters.
  • Spline.MonotoneConvex(), Spline.PCHIP() and Spline.Akima() switch formulas as knot rates change, and a minimum on a switch cannot be certified by derivatives. Prefer Spline.Linear() or Spline.Cubic() for more quotes than knots.
  • A model's variables stay strictly inside their intervals, and each needs an interval (lower .. upper). A fit whose minimum is on or beyond a bound, or too near one to tell, throws rather than return the bound.
  • Where the prices cannot be resolved to that tolerance the fit throws rather than stop short: quotes whose sizes differ by many orders of magnitude and share a parameter, a price that is a small difference of large amounts, or a quote priced at zero that the starting model also values at zero.
  • The certificate takes second derivatives of the prices. A pricer that is differentiable only to first order (a Hull–White swaption, or a model that runs a fit of its own) throws; use Fit.Loss. A model without variables (Yield.SmithWilson, which has a closed-form fit(model, quotes)) is a MethodError.

For FX.Forwards with a foreign spline or knot curve, the residuals are the quotes' own under the FX model, whatever mix of contracts they are. Derivatives with respect to the domestic curve are available for sets of outrights and par basis-swap legs. The tolerance on the Newton correction in an outright's net present value is at least four floating-point spacings at the size of the larger discounted leg. For a par outright the relative tolerance based on its starting or current value can give a larger allowance.

The minimum is a local one, and need not be the best fit of all parameters. optimizer and solve_kwargs belong to Fit.Loss and are not accepted. The solve works in Float64 at least: a model of Float32 numbers is returned with Float64 variables. Refitting a curve with BigFloat knot rates preserves that precision and uses the same stopping tolerances.

Fits are differentiable with ForwardDiff with respect to quote prices, the contract numbers listed under "Differentiating through a fit" in fit, the weights, and a Yield.FlatForwardAt forward, for any such model and any number of quotes. The derivatives are implicit ones, evaluated at the fitted parameters with the Hessian that certified them. A differentiated fit can throw when a repriced quote is at a turning point and a two-sided sensitivity cannot be resolved at the precision of the quoted price.

A subtype of FitMethod.

Examples

knots = [1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0]
c0 = ZeroRateCurve(fill(0.04, length(knots)), knots, Spline.Cubic())
curve = fit(c0, bond_quotes, Fit.LeastSquares())
weighted = fit(c0, bond_quotes, Fit.LeastSquares(weights = w))

FinanceModels.Fit.Loss — Type
Fit.Loss(function)

function should be a loss measure, such as x->x^2 or x->abs(x). This is used by the optimization algorithm in fit to determine optimal parameters as defined by this loss function.

A subtype of FitMethod.

Examples

julia> mod0 = Yield.Constant();

julia> quotes = ZCBPrice([0.9, 0.8, 0.7,0.6]);

julia> fit(mod0,quotes,Fit.Loss(x->x^2))
FinanceModels.Yield.Constant{Rate{Float64, Periodic}}(Periodic(0.12822921882254446, 1))

(With UnicodePlots loaded, fitted yield models display as a zero-rate chart instead.)

Interpolation Methods for ZeroRateCurve

ZeroRateCurve accepts an optional third argument specifying the interpolation method. The choice of interpolation affects forward curve smoothness, knot-rate sensitivity locality, and the cost of constructing and evaluating curves. Knot-rate sensitivity changes the interpolation inputs. ActuaryUtilities 6 KeyRates instead adds triangular continuous-zero shifts without refitting the curve; its shock shapes do not depend on the interpolant.

ZeroRateCurve returns a Yield.MonotoneConvex for Spline.MonotoneConvex() and a Yield.Spline for the other methods. Both are Yield.AbstractInterpolatedZeroCurves, as are the curves that spline fits return: read their knots with knot_rates and knot_tenors, and build a changed curve (other rates, tenors, method, or extrapolation) with reconstruct.

Available Methods

MethodZero-rate smoothness inside the knot gridKnot-rate localityDescription
Spline.MonotoneConvex()C1LocalDefault. Hagan–West interpolation with continuous, nonnegative forwards when the discrete forwards are nonnegative.
Spline.PCHIP()C1LocalPreserves monotonicity of the zero-rate inputs.
Spline.Akima()C1LocalUses nearby slopes to limit oscillation.
Spline.Linear()C0Adjacent intervalsSimple; instantaneous forwards jump at knots.
Spline.Quadratic()C1GlobalPiecewise quadratic zero rates.
Spline.Cubic()C2GlobalNatural cubic spline; a knot change affects the whole grid.
Spline.BSpline(d)Depends on degree and knotsGlobalDegree-d B-spline; not thread-safe for concurrent evaluation on a shared curve (shared internal buffer).

These smoothness statements concern tenor, not derivatives with respect to knot rates. C1 zero rates give continuous instantaneous forwards; they need not give differentiable forwards at the knots. Extrapolation and the short-end boundary can introduce additional forward jumps.

Since v6.0, Spline.Linear(), Spline.Quadratic(), and Spline.Cubic() return PolynomialSpline interpolants, which are fast and safe to evaluate concurrently across threads. Only Spline.Linear() is local; the quadratic and natural cubic splines are global. For a B-spline (e.g. as a least-squares fitting basis) request Spline.BSpline(d) explicitly; it is not thread-safe for concurrent evaluation on a shared curve.

Fit.Bootstrap() supports linear interpolation (Spline.Linear() or Spline.BSpline(1)): bootstrapping requires that each new knot leaves earlier segments unchanged. Fit other methods across the whole quote set with Fit.Loss.

using FinanceModels

rates = [0.02, 0.03, 0.035, 0.04, 0.045]
tenors = [1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0]

zrc = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors)                              # default: MonotoneConvex
zrc_pchip = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, Spline.PCHIP())        # PCHIP
zrc_lin = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, Spline.Linear())          # linear
zrc_cub = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, Spline.Cubic())           # natural cubic spline
zrc_aki = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, Spline.Akima())           # Akima
zrc_flat_zero = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, Spline.Cubic();
    extrapolation=:flat_zero)

knot_rates(zrc_lin)                                 # read-only view of the rates, as Continuous rates
zrc_up = reconstruct(zrc_lin; rates = rates .+ 0.001)   # every knot 10bp higher
zrc_mc = reconstruct(zrc_lin; spline = Spline.MonotoneConvex())

Before the First Knot

The DataInterpolations-backed methods hold the zero rate flat at the first knot's rate between t = 0 and the first knot. Spline.MonotoneConvex() instead interpolates from t = 0: the Hagan-West construction treats the origin as a node, with the instantaneous forward there set by its boundary condition. A bootstrapped curve's knots are exactly its quote maturities, and the flat short end prices the first quote's interval.

Extrapolation Beyond the Last Knot

Every knot-based curve defaults to extrapolation=:flat_forward: beyond the last knot (tₙ, zₙ) the instantaneous forward is held at a constant anchor fₙ, and for t > tₙ

\[z(t) = f_n + (z_n - f_n)\frac{t_n}{t}.\]

This preserves the discount factor at tₙ (discount factors are continuous there) and converges to fₙ as the horizon increases. The zero rate is not flat unless fₙ = zₙ. The anchor depends on the curve:

  • DataInterpolations-backed curves (Spline.Linear(), Quadratic(), Cubic(), PCHIP(), Akima(), BSpline(d), through Yield.Spline, ZeroRateCurve, or fit) anchor on the last discrete forward, the average continuously compounded forward over the last knot interval:

    \[f_n = \frac{z_n t_n - z_{n-1} t_{n-1}}{t_n - t_{n-1}}.\]

    (For a single knot, fₙ = z₁.) This anchor depends only on the last two knots and does not depend on the interpolant, so a quadratic, cubic, or B-spline end piece cannot push the tail to an extreme or negative level. The instantaneous forward generally jumps at tₙ, from the interpolant's endpoint forward zₙ + tₙz′(tₙ⁻) to fₙ.

  • Spline.MonotoneConvex() keeps its native boundary instantaneous forward f(tₙ) from the Hagan-West construction, so its forward curve is continuous at tₙ. That forward is built from the last discrete forwards and collared between 0 and twice the last discrete forward (when that forward is positive), so it is well behaved by construction.

For example, zero rates [0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035, 0.04] at [1, 2, 5, 10, 30] have a last discrete forward of 4.25%, which is the tail forward of every DataInterpolations-backed curve. Anchoring on the interpolant's endpoint derivative instead would make the tail depend on the interpolation method, and can produce negative or extreme long-end rates.

These are general curve-extension choices, not implementations of an accounting basis or prescribed regulatory extrapolation. For convergence to an independently chosen ultimate forward rate, see Yield.SmithWilson, which accepts ufr and a convergence-speed parameter α; model selection and calibration remain explicit choices.

Pass the extrapolation keyword to ZeroRateCurve, Yield.Spline, reconstruct, or a spline fit call to select the long-end behavior:

Yield.FlatForwardAt(r)f + (zₙ-f)tₙ/tIndependent forward assumption f, the continuously compounded value of the rate r; usually introduces a forward jump.

curve = Yield.Spline(Spline.Cubic(), tenors, rates; extrapolation=:flat_zero)
zrc = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, Spline.Cubic(); extrapolation=:linear)
quotes = ZCBYield.(Continuous.(rates), tenors)
fitted = fit(Spline.Linear(), quotes, Fit.Bootstrap(); extrapolation=:extension)

The selected value is available as curve.extrapolation on every knot curve. It is preserved by fitting, reconstruct, and Accessors.@set; derived boundary quantities are recomputed when knots change. Loss-fitting Spline.MonotoneConvex() returns a native Yield.MonotoneConvex, with Yield.instantaneous_forward, for every supported policy.

Yield.FlatForwardAt takes a rate with an explicit compounding convention:

assumed = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors; extrapolation=Yield.FlatForwardAt(Continuous(0.035)))
annual = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors; extrapolation=Yield.FlatForwardAt(Periodic(0.035, 1)))

A bare number (Yield.FlatForwardAt(0.035)) is a MethodError: Yield.Constant(0.035) reads a bare number as annual effective, so the convention must be stated. The rate is stored as its continuously compounded value and stays fixed when knot rates are bumped or fitted. Appending a synthetic far knot does not generally impose a chosen terminal instantaneous forward and can also change interior interpolation.

Key Tradeoffs

Forward Curve Smoothness

The instantaneous forward is f(t) = z(t) + t * z′(t). Linear zero-rate interpolation gives jumps at knots. PCHIP, MonotoneConvex, Akima, and Cubic give continuous interior forwards, although only the smoother choices also provide continuous forward derivatives. Check the required regularity when a model uses derivatives of the forward curve.

Use Yield.instantaneous_forward to evaluate the forward. At a jump it returns the right-hand value; a symmetric finite difference at the knot would instead average the two sides.

using FinanceModels

rates = [0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035, 0.04]
tenors = [1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0]
methods = [
    ("Linear", Spline.Linear()),
    ("PCHIP", Spline.PCHIP()),
    ("MonotoneConvex", Spline.MonotoneConvex()),
    ("Akima", Spline.Akima()),
    ("Cubic", Spline.Cubic()),
]
points = [1.99, 2.0, 2.01, 4.99, 5.0, 5.01]
forwards = map(methods) do (name, method)
    curve = ZeroRateCurve(rates, tenors, method)
    [rate(Yield.instantaneous_forward(curve, t)) for t in points]
end

MonotoneConvex preserves nonnegative continuous forwards when the input discrete forwards are nonnegative, under its default extrapolation. Other extrapolation policies can change the tail behavior. See Hagan & West (2006).

Knot-Rate Sensitivity Locality

Changing an interpolation knot can move rates beyond its adjacent intervals. The example below computes ∂z(t)/∂r₃, the sensitivity to the five-year continuous zero-rate input. It rebuilds the curve under each perturbation; it is not ActuaryUtilities' KeyRates shock.

using FinanceModels, ForwardDiff

rates = [0.02, 0.03, 0.035, 0.04, 0.045]
tenors = [1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0]
methods = [
    ("Linear", Spline.Linear()),
    ("PCHIP", Spline.PCHIP()),
    ("MonotoneConvex", Spline.MonotoneConvex()),
    ("Akima", Spline.Akima()),
    ("Cubic", Spline.Cubic()),
]
points = [0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0]
responses = map(methods) do (name, method)
    [ForwardDiff.gradient(r -> rate(zero(ZeroRateCurve(r, tenors, method), t)), rates)[3]
     for t in points]
end

Linear interpolation is local to the adjacent intervals inside the grid. The other methods spread the effect according to their slope construction. The relative magnitudes in this example depend on the input curve; they are not universal rankings. At each unperturbed knot the sensitivity is zero, because interpolation still passes through that knot.

Beyond the final knot, locality depends on the extrapolation policy as well. With :flat_forward, for fixed tenors,

\[\frac{\partial z(t)}{\partial r_i} = \frac{t_n}{t}\,\mathbf{1}_{i=n} + \left(1-\frac{t_n}{t}\right)\frac{\partial f_n}{\partial r_i}.\]

The anchor's sensitivities therefore persist throughout the tail. For the DataInterpolations-backed curves the anchor is the last discrete forward, so only the last two knots contribute, whatever the interpolant: ∂fₙ/∂rₙ = tₙ/(tₙ - tₙ₋₁) and ∂fₙ/∂rₙ₋₁ = -tₙ₋₁/(tₙ - tₙ₋₁). A narrow final interval amplifies both. MonotoneConvex's boundary forward depends on the last few discrete forwards. With FlatForwardAt(f) held fixed, only the final knot rate contributes to the extrapolated zero rate, with weight tₙ/t. These statements concern zero-rate sensitivities; cashflow PV sensitivities also depend on maturity and discounting.

Performance

Benchmark the operation you actually need: repeated evaluation, rebuilding curves from perturbed knots, and differentiating through calibration have different costs. Precompile the operation before timing it, and keep the same inputs and derivative order when comparing methods.

In ActuaryUtilities 6, fixed-cashflow sensitivities uses analytic derivatives of its continuous-zero shocks. Callback valuations use ForwardDiff. sensitivities(...) defaults to first order; request sensitivities(SecondOrder(), ...) for convexity. Neither a KeyRates grid nor a callback automatically rebuilds the interpolation knots. To measure knot-rate risk, reconstruct the curve inside the valuation, as above.

Choosing a Method

  • Spline.MonotoneConvex() is the default for its treatment of forwards. Its nonnegative-forward guarantee depends on nonnegative input discrete forwards and the extrapolation policy.
  • Spline.PCHIP() preserves monotonic zero-rate data with local slope construction.
  • Spline.Linear() gives localized knot effects within the grid when forward jumps are acceptable.
  • Spline.Akima() offers another local slope construction; inspect the resulting forwards on your data.
  • Spline.Cubic() gives smoother interior forwards, with global knot effects and possible overshoot.

For every method, inspect the short end, the knots, and the extrapolation tail. A curve suitable for pricing the calibration instruments may still need a different interpolation or extrapolation choice for another use.
